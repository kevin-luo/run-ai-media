# Creator pattern library

Use these as composable functions, not identities to impersonate.

## Chinese-language creators

### 歸藏 / @op7418

- Opens with an unfiltered reaction, then immediately names the product or result.
- Shows the artifact: screenshot, video, quoted source, or before/after.
- Explains why the demo is hard or surprising, so readers know what to notice.
- Best borrowed function: **reaction + evidence + why it matters**.
- Risk to avoid: empty superlatives when the proof is weak.

### 宝玉 / @dotey

- Finds the social subtext around technical news and compresses it into one sharp line.
- Uses quote posts and screenshots so the reader can see the joke or contradiction.
- Best borrowed function: **one-line interpretation of a source artifact**.
- Risk to avoid: commentary that requires insider context the audience does not have.

### 索螺丝 / @fiapp_pro

- Asks concrete questions about products, interfaces, workflows, and business incentives.
- Leaves the tension unresolved, which invites practitioners to answer.
- Best borrowed function: **specific industry question with real stakes**.
- Risk to avoid: vague engagement bait.

### 图拉鼎 / @tualatrix

- Posts a real action in progress: installing an OS, paying a bill, shipping a tool, meeting someone.
- The photo carries credibility; the text stays short and lets readers infer the story.
- Slips product progress into lived experience instead of announcing a campaign.
- Best borrowed function: **small lived detail + real image + open loop**.
- Risk to avoid: manufacturing a lifestyle anecdote.

### 管四 / @guansi

- Begins with a personal oddity noticed during use.
- Compares products through felt differences before explaining a larger trend.
- Best borrowed function: **first-person observation + comparison + tentative conclusion**.
- Risk to avoid: turning one impression into a universal fact.

## English-language creators

### Ethan Mollick / @emollick

- Turns fast-moving releases into opinionated decision guidance.
- Uses one clear claim, one concrete example, and an invitation to try something stranger.
- Best borrowed function: **practical judgment under uncertainty**.
- Risk to avoid: model rankings without task context.

### Simon Willison / @simonw

- Anchors every claim in a version number, test result, failure count, repository, or reproducible note.
- Links to the primary artifact and explains what changed in his own projects.
- Best borrowed function: **specific evidence + reproducible consequence**.
- Risk to avoid: detail without a reader-facing takeaway.

### swyx / @swyx

- Spots ecosystem signals early and translates them into developer or market implications.
- Uses compressed, conversational language and highlights one number or neglected workflow.
- Best borrowed function: **weak signal + strong implication + actionable edge**.
- Risk to avoid: overconfident extrapolation from one metric.

### Andrej Karpathy / @karpathy

- Alternates deep technical explanation with dry, compact humor.
- Lets the setup stay ordinary and puts the twist in the final clause.
- Best borrowed function: **technical credibility with a low-key punchline**.
- Risk to avoid: trying too hard to sound clever.

## Composite formats

1. **Fresh release**
   - Reaction from 歸藏
   - Source discipline from Simon Willison
   - Reader decision from Ethan Mollick

2. **Practical workflow**
   - Concrete steps from 索螺丝
   - Lived failure from 图拉鼎
   - Dry closing line from Karpathy

3. **Model comparison**
   - Personal observation from 管四
   - Task-specific evidence from Simon Willison
   - Clear recommendation from Ethan Mollick

4. **Quote post**
   - Source artifact from 宝玉
   - One ecosystem implication from swyx
   - Stop after the punchline.
