# Ongoing account playbook

Use this reference for recurring account operation or publishing while the owner is away.

## Optimize for

1. Trust: claims have visible proof or a primary source.
2. Discovery: timely replies put the account in relevant conversations.
3. Memory: recurring formats make the account recognizable.
4. Conversion: profile visits understand the account promise quickly.

Do not chase activity for its own sake. A skipped slot is better than a generic post.

## Daily rhythm for a small AI account

- Publish one proof-led main post: a demo, real workflow, failure, benchmark, repository, or product artifact.
- Publish at most one timely reaction when the account has a distinct angle.
- Add 4–6 useful replies across the day. Spread them across creators and topics.
- Avoid more than two original posts in 24 hours until the account has enough audience signal to justify a higher cadence.

Treat these as ceilings, not quotas.

## Find candidates

Check primary announcements, fast-moving AI conversations, developer releases, and real product demos. Score each candidate from 0–2 on:

- recency;
- fit with AI product practice;
- available proof;
- room to add something new;
- visual or demonstration value.

Work on candidates scoring at least 7/10. Skip a topic when the only contribution is a summary.

## Choose the response

- Reply when one sharp detail or joke is enough.
- Quote-post when the source artifact must remain visible and the account has a clear interpretation.
- Write a main post when the account owns the test, artifact, workflow, or analysis.
- Use a thread only for a genuinely sequential tutorial.

Replies must add a detail, question, comparison, correction, or practitioner joke. Never leave praise-only replies such as “Great work” or “This changes everything.”

## Publish while the owner is away

The standing permission covers normal AI product and developer-tool content. In unattended runs:

- use a primary source for release claims;
- post only claims that can be checked from the source or the account's supplied artifact;
- avoid politics, personal disputes, medical, legal, financial, and safety-sensitive advice;
- do not reveal private files, private conversations, credentials, unpublished product details, or personal contact information;
- do not make purchase commitments, promise partnerships, or speak on behalf of another person;
- skip publishing when sources conflict, the account session is missing, or the angle feels generic.

If publishing fails, save the exact draft and source. Do not repeatedly submit the same post.

## Write for a global timeline

- Avoid local-only phrases such as “tonight” and “this morning.”
- Use “now available,” “the weights are scheduled for July 28,” or an exact timezone when the timing is essential.
- Do not assume readers share the account owner's location.

## Preserve human voice

- Start from the oddest true detail, not the topic label.
- Use one concrete object or failure in every practical post.
- Explain technical nouns in ordinary language on first mention.
- Earn the joke from the situation. Keep one punchline and stop.
- Delete any closing sentence that merely restates the post.
- Use the creator library as a set of skills: borrow one person's evidence discipline, another person's opener, and a third person's restraint.
- Run the product-name swap test before publishing. Generic sentences fail.
- Keep the slightly spoken version when it is clearer than the polished version.

## Final publish gate

Do not publish until every answer is yes:

- Can a reader point to the proof?
- Does the account add something beyond the source?
- Is every technical phrase understandable on first read?
- Does the copy contain a real sentence the owner might say aloud?
- Is the joke tied to this exact situation?
- Would removing the final sentence make the post stronger? If yes, remove it.

## Record every action

For each published item, store:

- timestamp and public URL;
- exact text and attached media;
- source links;
- format, hook, proof type, and topic;
- 2-hour and 24-hour reach, replies, saves, profile visits, and follows when available.

Review after ten original posts. Keep formats that repeatedly earn profile visits, saves, or substantive replies; do not judge a format from a single post.
